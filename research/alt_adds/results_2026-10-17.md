## 1. ONE WORKED EXAMPLE

EURUSD, a long opened at 1.1600 (an hourly anchor), exit 9 pips. If the market falls to 1.1503 before
it ever trades at 1.1609, the excursion is 97 pips: an OPT stack at add 7, cap 8 (49 pips) would have
rolled; an ALT stack at add 16, cap 7 (96 pips) would just have held. Over all 181 EURUSD anchors, 90%
of the long side's excursions were 96.8 pips or less: that is where 16 comes from.

## 3. WHAT IT SHOWS

- **The live OPT stacks are overrun often:** 7-46% of anchors per side would have rolled. EURGBP long is
  the extreme (46%): its daily range is the smallest of the 21 pairs (21 pips), but it TRENDED down in
  early October, and a 21-pip stack cannot hold a trend. The first guess (s8, from daily ranges) put
  EURGBP's ALT at 7; our own fills say 14. Daily range is not what decides a roll; one-way distance is.
- **One side per pair carries it** (the trend side of the fortnight): EURUSD L, GBPUSD S (16 Oct's
  rally), EURGBP L, AUDCAD L, AUDCHF S then L. Per-side adds would fit tighter; one add per pair is
  proposed (simpler, and next fortnight's trend side is unknown).
- **The last week is wider than the fortnight on GBPUSD, AUDCAD, AUDCHF** (q90 up 1.5-2.3x), narrower on
  EURUSD, EURGBP, CADCHF.

## 4. ALT'S EXIT MOVES THE ANSWER AS MUCH AS THE ADD

With ALT's exit = its proposed add (a bigger pullback needed before the side clears), q90 over the whole
period grows: EURUSD L 96.8 -> 115.1, GBPUSD S 82.0 -> 109.1, EURGBP L 83.5 -> 97.8, AUDCAD L 63.3 ->
148.5, AUDCHF S 78.2 -> 79.2 (L 69.3 -> 85.3), CADCHF unchanged (exit 9 both). So the stacks above cover
about 80-90% with exit = add, 90% with exit = OPT's. **Claude's recommendation: ALT's exit = OPT's exit
for the first build** (one change at a time; the basis these adds were measured on). The operator's call.
