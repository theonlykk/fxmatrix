# ADR-119: V2 Circuit Breaker Trigger A v1 -- Operational Anomaly Signals

## Status

Accepted -- 2026-08-17. Gemini ruling ("Trigger A v1 Implementation & S3
Tension"). Shipped at commit 17c1843; unit suite 730/730 at ship (9 TA tests:
5 pure-logic + 4 wired behavioural). Dormant on origin -- not deployed to the
VPS (which remains on 1c6797e). Follows ADR-118 (Trigger B) per the phased
rollout.

## Context

ADR-118 shipped Trigger B (the equity floor) as the effect-side net -- it
catches runaway LOSS. Trigger A is the cause-side net: it catches the
OPERATIONAL FAULT that would cause runaway behaviour early, so a human can
restart and clean before loss accumulates. This is the fault Khalid actually
fears -- "a technological mishap could blow the account when all it needed was
a restart and clean."

Gemini ruled (Q1) a phased rollout: Trigger B first, then Trigger A with only
DETERMINISTIC signals (Q4) -- explicitly excluding rate-based or fuzzy-logic
anomalies, because a false-positive operational halt during a fast, highly-
liquid market is unacceptable. The response reuses the existing pause-and-hold
halt; Trigger A stays INDEPENDENT of the BCC, preserving BCC's ratified
passive-detect-only identity (Trigger A is the active safety layer).

One tension inside the ruling required resolution. Q4 lists "strict position-
vs-managed-layer divergence" as a v1 signal AND mandates no false-positive
halts. A naive strict per-tick compare violates the latter: in MT5 the terminal
order book and the EA's internal layer arrays update on different ticks, so
managed depth and broker position count diverge TRANSIENTLY on essentially
every fill. Sampling mid-state would halt on normal operation.

## Decision

Add three deterministic pause-and-hold signals as a new shared-engine module
(fxmatrix_v2_trigger_a.mqh), wired at OnTick, gated on InpTaEnable. Per
Gemini's rulings:

- S1 hard-max layer depth (SIDE-LOCAL): trip when ArraySize(g_*_layers) >
  InpMaxLayers. Adds are gated at n >= InpMaxLayers, so exceeding it is a by-
  design impossibility -- a clean runaway signal. Halts only the offending
  side.
- S2 repeated same-direction CloseBy CRITICAL (ACCOUNT-WIDE, count >= 2): a
  single same-direction exhaustion already halts its side (existing exits.mqh
  behaviour, preserved); a SECOND occurrence confirms a systemic CloseBy loop
  and escalates account-wide via the Trigger B V2CB_ACCT_HALT GV. The counter
  is incremented at the exits.mqh CRITICAL site; escalation is decided in the
  engine (include order forbids calling the CB GV from exits.mqh). Idempotent:
  publishes once, then re-asserts both halts silently.
- S3 position-vs-managed divergence (SIDE-LOCAL, 2-CHECK DEBOUNCE): compare
  managed depth to the broker position count for the side's entry magic at
  END of OnTick (after fills, CloseBy, and the BCC sweep settle). Gemini ruled
  Option A -- require the divergence to persist across 2 consecutive checks
  before halting, reusing the deterministic 2-sweep debounce already approved
  for BCC. This neutralises the MT5 async fill transient without introducing
  fuzzy logic.

Scope mapping follows Q6: side-local for state inconsistencies (S1, S3),
account-wide for systemic execution failures (S2). Resume is manual only.
Pure-logic cores are separated for hermetic tests, and behavioural tests drive
the WIRED detectors through a mock-broker hook to verify the halt flags, scope
(side-local vs account-wide), the debounce (transient does not halt; two
consecutive do), and S2 idempotency.

## Consequences

Positive:
- The account now has both circuit-breaker nets: Trigger A catches the
  operational cause early (pause-and-hold + alert -> human restart/clean),
  Trigger B catches the loss effect as a last resort. Neither ever flattens.
- Deterministic-only signals honour the no-false-positive mandate; the S3
  debounce is the one subtlety and is transient-immune by construction.
- BCC stays passive; the active safety layer is cleanly decoupled.
- Coverage is behavioural, not just arithmetic: the wired detectors, mock-
  broker path, and scope are tested, so a future regression in the halt wiring
  fails the suite.

Negative / bounded:
- v1 ships only the three unambiguous signals; fuzzy/rate-based anomalies are
  deliberately deferred until a boring, low-false-positive definition exists.
- S3's broker count reads the live account book in production (mocked in
  tests); the debounce is what makes that safe.
- Dormant until deployed. No protection until deploy.ps1 ships it to the VPS.
