## 2. ROOT CAUSE: ADR-123 WAS LOST IN THE fxgrind REWRITE

From `send_logs` (ADR-131 archive), the composition was unambiguous:

- **EVERY send was `action=MODIFY`.** No PENDING, no CLOSE_BY, no trades.
- 13 real sends (retcode 10009, 93-109 ms) in **49 seconds** from 4
  instances.
- GBPUSD L0 chasing mid: 1.35198 -> 1.35115 -> 1.35172 -> 1.35116 in ~50 s.

**ADR-123 (Accepted 2026-08-28, Gemini ruling) states verbatim:**

> "The dumb straddle should never re-quote. It posts two limits and waits."
> "Place-once-and-wait: ... If already resting, leave it untouched."

and explicitly REJECTED the alternative that was running:

> "Per-order deadband on Replace: unnecessary when the straddle never
> re-quotes; adds complexity without benefit."

`Grind_TryPlaceL0` did exactly the rejected thing: found the resting L0,
checked a 4-pip deadband, and MODIFIED when the target had moved further.

**How it happened:** ADR-125 decision 7 cites three inherited behaviours in
one line -- "Place-once straddle (ADR-123). Fill-triggered re-center
(ADR-124) via OrderModify. Absolute-pip deadband (ADR-121/122)." Those
partly contradict each other. fxgrind implemented the deadband and LOST the
"fill-triggered" qualifier. **ADR-124 was a stub** -- ADR-125's references
line called it "re-centering -- reserved write-up" -- so the rule governing
live behaviour was never written down.

### 2a. IT COST FILLS, NOT JUST REQUESTS (operator's insight)

A chasing buy limit RETREATS as price approaches it. L0 sits 5 pips below
mid; price falls 4; the order re-quotes 4 lower. For a fill, price must
travel the full width WITHIN ONE TICK, because anything slower re-centres
the quote out of reach. A STATIC order fills on CUMULATIVE drift at any
speed.

This explains why 8 pairs traded NOTHING all session while prices moved. The
only two that laddered were EURGBP (width 3, tightest) and AUDCHF -- and
once L0 fills, ADDS are anchored to the filled entry and do NOT chase.

### 2b. THE ARCHITECTURAL BOUNDARY (Gemini, ratified)

**Re-quote on INFORMATION, never on NOISE.**

- Both sides flat: mid fluctuation is NOISE. The market has absorbed no
  liquidity, so there is no reason to move our quotes. **ADR-123.**
- A layer fills: the market has revealed conviction and the book has
  materially changed. Re-centering the opposite, untraded side is
  INFORMATION-driven. **ADR-124.**

## 5. TWO FURTHER DEFECTS FOUND DURING RECOVERY

### 5a. ADR-139 -- I6 FLOATING-POINT BOUNDARY HALT (fixed, merged `b2e4689`)

AUDCHF_OPT halted `I6_SHORT_EXIT` during reconstruction. Layer S/L01 entry
0.58531, exit_pips 5 -> expected exit 0.58481. The filled exit position sat
at 0.58479, **exactly 2 points away**.

    MathAbs(0.58481 - 0.58479) = 2.0000000000020002e-05
    2 * point                  = 2e-05
    fails by                     2e-17

**Pure binary representation error, not slippage.** Exit prices land on
exact pip boundaries constantly, so roughly half of all boundary
comparisons lose.

Fix: `GRIND_PRICE_EPS 1e-9` added to `grind_pure.mqh`, applied to the I6
comparison only. 1e-9 is far below one point (1e-5) so it cannot mask a real
breach. Tests FB1-FB4.

**Gemini reviewed and cleared the three neighbouring comparisons:**
`Grind_PriceWithinDeadband` uses a strict `<` (fails safe by re-quoting);
the Adr013 clamps compare against `point * 0.1` with a strict `>` (fails
safe by reading "did not move").

**Cost before the fix:** the halt blocked CloseBy on two netted layers and
forced four manual position closes.

### 5b. THE TEST SUITE WAS NOT REPRODUCIBLE (fixed, merged `df7b12e`)

Commit `fc46d2f` reported **910/910 at 20:25 and 909/910 later the same
evening**. Same commit, different result.

**Cause:** MQL5 GlobalVariables PERSIST in the terminal between script runs
and are written to disk when the terminal closes. The suite wrote many and
cleaned only magic locks.

Fix: `Test_SuiteResetGlobals()` deletes every GV whose name starts with
`GRIND`, iterating DOWNWARD (delete reindexes; an ascending loop skips
alternate entries), called FIRST and LAST in `OnStart`, printing the count.

**Verified: four consecutive runs, 919/920 and "cleared 21" every time.**

**Why this matters more than any single test:** I quoted 910/910 as evidence
on three separate merges tonight. At least one of those numbers was
unreliable. From now a SUMMARY line is evidence.

## 8. WHAT THE FIX MEANS FOR EVERYTHING ELSE

**Every sweep ever run assumes L0 re-quoting.**
`grid_sim_v7_real_signal.py` recomputes `bar_start_quotes` from each bar's
opening price, i.e. it re-quotes L0 EVERY 5-MINUTE BAR, and pays nothing for
it. So:

- the live EA re-quoted far FASTER than the sim (per tick vs per bar);
- the ratified behaviour (place once, never) is what NEITHER models;
- **the geometry selected by every sweep to date was chosen under a fill
  model that matches neither the ruling nor the code.**

Gemini ruled **CONFORM AND COMPARE**: let the running 13-pair carry-corrected
sweep finish as a baseline, then update the simulator to model place-once
(L0 anchored at placement, moving only on a modelled stranded breach), and
run a focused comparison. He expects place-once to materially alter which
geometries score best.

This may also explain why live fill rates have fallen short of simulated
ones.
