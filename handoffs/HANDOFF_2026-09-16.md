## 1. THE FINDING: THE ACCOUNT LIMIT REFUSES EXITS AND HALTS INSTANCES

**The limit is 200, and on this account it binds on POSITIONS + PENDING
ORDERS combined.** Read directly with a read-only script
(`MQL5\Scripts\AccountLimits.mq5` on the VPS, saved and safe to re-run):

    LIMIT_ORDERS=200 positions=81 orders=113 margin_mode=2

MT5 documentation describes `ACCOUNT_LIMIT_ORDERS` as a pending-order limit.
113 pending is nowhere near 200, and the refusals happened with the total at
the limit, so the evidence says total. Trust the evidence, not the docs.

**Mechanism, proven for two of three halts:**

  1. An entry fills. The filled order becomes a position (count unchanged),
     then the EA places an exit and the next add (+2).
  2. At the limit, the EXIT is refused: retcode 10040, `duration_ms=0`
     (the terminal refuses locally; the request never reaches the broker).
  3. The layer has no exit -> `I3_*_NAKED` -> quarantine (ADR-128,
     3000 ms / 3 checks).
  4. The limit does not clear in 3 s -> `QUARANTINE_HALT`.

**The limit refuses the one order the strategy cannot do without.** Resting
entries and adds already hold their slots; the new exit is what gets turned
away.

### Residual ALT book and the Close By pairs

On this HEDGING account an exit does NOT close its position -- it opens an
opposite position, and the EA normally nets them with CloseBy. Detached, nobody
does. When an ALT exit fills you will see an opposite pair with P&L locked,
still holding 2 slots. Free them with Close By on EXACTLY these tickets (a
position's ticket equals its opening order's ticket):

| symbol | ALT position | exit order (Close By partner once filled) |
|---|---|---|
| AUDNZD | 543389462 buy 1.23871 | 543404320 sell 1.23941 |
| AUDNZD | 543230539 buy 1.24011 | 543249326 sell 1.24081 |
| NZDCAD | 543282840 sell 0.80275 | 543479452 buy 0.80205 |
| NZDCAD | 543240533 sell 0.80174 | 543282830 buy 0.80104 |
| NZDCAD | 543229824 sell 0.80074 | 543240485 buy 0.80004 |

**Close By against an OPT position would remove it from OPT's book and halt
OPT.** OPT trades within pips of these prices. Match on ticket, never price.

**If closing residual ALT positions at market instead:** delete that
position's paired exit order in the same step. Otherwise the exit keeps its
slot and can later open a position with nothing to net it.

### Lower the layer cap

Operator view: scalps earn, waiting for open losses to recover does not;
prefers risk spread across more pairs to GBPUSD holding ~36 slots.

  - A lower cap frees nothing immediately; it limits growth.
  - `max_layers` has never been swept. Harvest effect unknown. Every
    tail-gate breach in the 09-15 sweeps had the cap saturated at 8, so a
    lower cap may also help Gate A.
  - Candidate: majors 12 -> 7, crosses 8 -> 7; 6 later. A geometry change:
    ADR required; DeepSeek normally mandatory; operator may overrule on demo.

**Two traps, from source:**

  1. `I7_LONG/SHORT_DEPTH` fires when depth > `max_layers`, and it is NOT
     quarantinable -> immediate halt on reinit. Both GBPUSD arms were at depth
     7: a cap of 6 halts them.
  2. `Grind_EnsureAddNext` keeps an already-resting add whose label matches
     the next index, even at cap; it only blocks NEW placement. If that add
     fills, depth = cap + 1 -> I7 halt. Delete the resting add at the new cap
     index right after reinit (the EA will not re-place it at cap). Both
     GBPUSD arms had L07 adds resting (543497956, 543737360).

**Measure first:** share of scalp income from layers >= 6 (scalps endpoint
carries layer depth). If negligible, a cap of 6 is nearly free.

### Reset vs never stop out -- OPEN

Operator floated closing deep underwater layers when a new range is obviously
established far away, then said he likes the mantra of never stopping out and
that a lower cap may make resets unnecessary. **Recorded as an open design
question, not an EA change.** If resets ever become repeatable they need a
written trigger (e.g. time at cap, distance beyond the last layer) and a clean
flatten mechanism -- closing by hand under an attached EA breaks its book.
