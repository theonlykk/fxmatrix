## 1. WHAT WAS DECIDED

**ADR-152 (draft, `cdb95f4`)** consolidates entry purgatory. Three decisions:
fill-time placement via a single-attempt optimistic lock; a reserved guard band
(near-market entries get the full 194, others stop at 194 - Q, Q = 8); and the
entry horizon itself (place at 2 x add_pips, cancel at 4 x add_pips, with a
one-step floor that ALWAYS rests). Plus a per-side transition budget, a hard
entry stop at 1,900 requests/day, the full flag lifecycle, and an explicit gap
policy. Two-phase rollout with operator fallbacks and promotion gates.

**ADR-152a is abandoned (`27f55fa`)**, never implemented. It had been drafted to
Gemini's first ruling (isolate the fill-path timing change as a prerequisite).
The teardown killed it on two counts, both verified in source: a due flag
serviced next tick only reorders work inside that tick and does not remove the
tick boundary, so the motivating repro still misses; and servicing due adds
before L0 lets a far add take the last guard unit ahead of an at-market L0 --
the starvation ADR-152 exists to remove. Gemini accepted the fold (Q4-bis) and
the optimistic lock (Q7) with one hard amendment: **the fill-handler lock path
must contain zero retry loops and zero `Sleep()` calls.**

## 2. THREE SOURCE FACTS THAT CHANGED THE DESIGN

F1. **Adds are not place-once.** `Grind_EnsureAddNext` (grind_engine.mqh
    ~772-777) calls `Grind_ModifyPendingPrice` when a pending add's clamped
    target leaves the deadband (`InpDeadbandPips = 4.0`). Add churn already
    exists; purgatory changes its shape, it does not introduce it.
F2. **The ADR-013 clamp never reaches the market.** `Grind_Adr013ClampBuy`
    (grind_pure.mqh:149-161) returns `min(theoretical, bid - min_dist)` and
    `Grind_BuyLimitMarketable` (:137) is MISNAMED -- it returns true when the
    limit is PASSIVE. A clamped add rests below the bid and fills on the next
    downtick. Today's fast foothold fills were falling-market fills, not market
    orders. Correct any note that says otherwise.
F3. **Exits are already sent from `OnTradeTransaction`**
    (`Grind_HandleSideDealFill` -> `Grind_ExitQManageSide`, ~1008), and exits do
    NOT take the slot lock. So sending an entry from the fill handler is not a
    new class of risk; the only real objection was `Grind_SlotLockAcquire`
    sleeping up to 50 ms (grind_exitq.mqh:173-198). Hence the single-attempt
    lock.
