## 4. THE CARRY CORRECTION -- THE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING TODAY

### 4a. The sweep does not model financing at all

`sim_costs.py` models spread and commission only. Every sweep result ever
produced overstates P&L by the carry paid on held layers.

**The bias is NOT uniform.** Wider exits hold longer, so exit=7 cells are
flattered more than exit=2. The sweep's SHAPE conclusions -- which geometry
wins -- may invert once carry is applied.

Worked example: a 5-pip scalp held 10 days on AUDJPY costs 5.43 pips of
carry. The trade loses money even when it wins.

### 4b. The approximation that makes historical windows tractable

Swap decomposes as:

```
long  = +differential - markup
short = -differential - markup
=> markup = -(long + short) / 2 ;  differential = (long - short) / 2
```

**For a two-sided market maker the differential CANCELS.** The average of
long and short carry is exactly `-markup`. The differential is what varies
wildly across eras (near-zero in 2020, wide today); the MARKUP is a broker
pricing property and far more stable. So:

```
carry_cost_pips  ~=  hold_nights x markup(pair)
```

Measured markups (pips/night): NZDCAD 0.203, NZDCHF 0.272, NZDJPY 0.275,
CADCHF 0.288, CADJPY 0.294, EURGBP 0.344, AUDNZD 0.382, AUDCAD 0.382,
AUDCHF 0.398, EURUSD 0.419, CHFJPY 0.441, GBPUSD 0.498, AUDJPY 0.543.
Range 0.20-0.54, mean 0.36.

No policy-rate history needed. Caveats: in a trending window the ladder
leans one way so the differential does not fully cancel within that window
(markup is the expectation, not the realised value); and historical markups
are unverifiable from today's data.

### 4c. What to do (three tasks, in order)

1. **Post-hoc correction of the running sweep.** Do NOT stop it. It reports
   mean hold time per cell, so `hold_nights x markup` can be applied
   afterwards to rank cells honestly. Spreadsheet-level work once the JSON
   lands.
2. **Add carry to `sim_costs.py`** as a real cost alongside spread and
   commission, using rollover counting (midnight crossings, skipping
   Sat/Sun, x3 on the broker's triple day -- V1's `RunCarryRecalculation`
   already implements exactly this counting). Then re-run.
3. **Apply the same lens to the LIVE fleet.** See 4d.

## 5. ORDER OCCUPANCY -- THE 272 ESTIMATE WAS WRONG

Operator's correction: layers stack when the market TRENDS, and the market
cannot trend up and down at once. Deep stacks on both sides simultaneously
would need price to travel far down and far back up without touching any
exit, which the geometry rules out (exits sit 5-10 pips from entry).

Per side: N layers = N exit orders, plus one pending (L0 when flat, add
otherwise), none at the cap. So worst case per instance is
**8 exits + 1 pending on the flat side = 9**, not 16.

| Fleet | Instances | Orders (worst) | Requests/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 pairs x 2 arms (today) | 12 | 108 | ~1,200 |
| 3+5 ring x 2 arms | 16 | 144 | ~1,600 |
| 6 pairs x 3 arms | 18 | 162 | ~1,800 |
| 3+5 ring x 3 arms | 24 | 216 (OVER) | ~2,400 (OVER) |

Order cap binds at 22 instances; the REQUEST budget binds first, at 20.
Observed reality is ~3 orders per instance (peak ~36 across 12).

**Consequence: order purgatory stays deferred.** It is not needed for the
3+5 ring in both arms. It becomes necessary only for a third ring or all
seven new pairs in both arms.

**Unbuilt, cheap insurance:** an `ea_event` when an instance's resting-order
count crosses ~12. Nothing would fire today; it is silent until the model
above is wrong.

## 6. THE 3+5 RING (operator's preferred shape)

Ring of 3: EURUSD, GBPUSD, EURGBP.
Ring of 5: CADCHF, CHFJPY, AUDJPY, AUDNZD, NZDCAD.

**Why rings:** in a closed cycle every currency appears in exactly two
pairs, which is what makes the leg-cap logic uniform. A pentagon preserves
that; eight pairs over five currencies would not (each currency would appear
four times).

**Consequences to plan for:**
- **AUDCHF and AUDCAD are chords, not edges** -- they leave the fleet. Four

- **CADCHF is the only survivor** of the current second triangle and already

- **AUDJPY is quoted AGAINST the direction of travel** (the traversal wants

  account for it or exposures will not net around the ring.
- **CHFJPY is the weakest candidate so far**: widest spread (1.30), only

## 7. SCALING ROADMAP -- THE UNIT IS THE ACCOUNT, NOT THE LOT

### 7a. The operator's decisive point

All drawdown testing, every stress window and the entire A/B were measured
at **0.01 lots on a 10k account**. Running 0.05 on the same account is not
scaling -- it is a 5x leverage change with no testing behind it. The tested
unit is the ACCOUNT.

Multiple 10k accounts at 0.01 each preserve the tested risk profile AND
multiply every constraint that binds: order cap, request budget and
drawdown allowance are all per account. Partial fills never arise.

### 7b. Partial fills: the problem, and why it is not a solved problem here

At 0.01 a fill is binary. `GrindLayer` has NO volume field; exits are placed
for the `InpLots` input, not the position's actual volume.

**`TRADE_ACTION_MODIFY` cannot change a pending order's volume** (price,
SL, TP, expiry only). Gemini: 100% confidence; either silently ignored with
`10009 DONE` or rejected with `10014 INVALID_VOLUME`. **UNVERIFIED on this
terminal** -- see the Monday test in section 9.

Four designs considered, three rejected:

1. **Atomise the entry** (ten 0.01 orders for 0.10): 1,280 resting orders
   at 16 instances vs a 200 cap. Dead.
2. **Cancel and replace the exit per incremental fill**: 1,280 requests vs
   a 2,000 budget with 1,170 used. Dead.
3. **Wait for `ORDER_STATE_FILLED` before placing the exit**: cheap, but
   violates the operator's constraint that every filled unit must have a
   resting exit IMMEDIATELY -- otherwise a snap-back is missed, which is the
   move the system exists to harvest. Rejected.
4. **Place per fill, consolidate later under order-count pressure**:
   provisional survivor.

**Gemini's CloseBy finding, with a correction.** He claimed a partial fill
creates multiple entry POSITION tickets. Claude believes this is wrong: a
position's ticket equals its opening ORDER's ticket, and later deals from
the same order accumulate into the SAME position, which grows. But the
underlying point stands and inverts his conclusion:

| Design | CloseBy pairing | Residual? |
|---|---|---|
| One exit per fill slice | matched volumes, 1:1 | **no** |
| One consolidated exit | 1 exit vs N entry slices | **yes, cascading** |

**Consolidation is what creates the residual cascade**, not partial fills.
Residuals carry NEW tickets, which breaks the layer identity the tracker
depends on. So the trade-off is: unconsolidated exits keep CloseBy clean and
blow the order cap; consolidated exits bound orders and break the tracker.

**Nothing is built. The ordering is measure-first** (see 9).

### 7c. Hosting economics

VPS is 2 vCPU / 4 GB / Windows 2022, Chicago, ~$60/month with the Windows
licence. At session time: 2.9/4.0 GB used (72%) with the market CLOSED.

But MT5 itself is only **257 MB with twelve EAs attached**. The rest is
Windows (~1.7 GB kernel/cache) plus processes only running because the
operator is logged in: Task Manager 236 MB, RDP 190 MB, MetaEditor 92 MB,
Server Manager 60 MB. Closing those frees ~570 MB; a second terminal with
16 EAs needs ~340 MB.

So two accounts probably fit on this box; three probably do not. **Re-check
terminal memory MID-SESSION on a weekday** -- Saturday numbers understate.

**A dead VPS does not pause the strategy.** Resting orders stay live at the
broker, so adds keep filling with no EA to place their exits -- the naked
position state the invariants exist to catch. That is the real argument for
separate failure domains, stronger than resource contention.

**Rejected:** Raspberry Pi (RAM prices; also ARM needs box64 + Wine, two
emulation layers, plus home latency and power/ISP failure domain). Surface
as a permanent host (sleep, Wi-Fi, Windows Update, battery; and it is
already the sweep machine).

**Worth a POC: MT5 on Linux under Wine.** MetaQuotes publish an install
path. Removes the Windows licence, which is the entire cost difference
(~$60 -> ~$15/box). Needs: connection stability over a week, WebRequest with
the URL allowlist, a Linux equivalent of `deploy.ps1` (PowerShell today),
and memory under twelve EAs.

### 7d. FTMO's Scaling Plan is the sanctioned path

Balance grows on the SAME account after meeting profit criteria. No
partial-fill engine, no second VPS, no extra order or request budget, and
the risk profile stays the tested one: at 20k, 0.02 lots is the same
relative risk as 0.01 at 10k. **Read the actual criteria** -- it changes what
"scaling up" should mean for this project.

### 7e. A third arm (OPT, ALT, ALT2)

Fits on six pairs (18 instances, 162 orders, ~1,800 requests) but NOT with
the pentagon (24 instances, over both caps). Against it: the arms are highly
correlated (same pairs, same direction, same moments), so a third arm is
~1.5x the exposure of two against the same drawdown limit; and it dilutes
the experiment. **The version that would earn its place is a different KIND
of trade** -- wider geometry, longer horizon -- not a third exit distance.

### 7f. Wide geometry (the "longer sweep")

Example: width 20 / exit 15, far outside the swept grid (widths 3-7, exits
2-7). Costs shrink relative to the target: CADCHF spread is 22% of a 5-pip
exit but 7% of a 15-pip one; a week-held AUDCHF short is 149% of a 5-pip
target but 50% of a 15-pip one. Against it: fill rate scales roughly as
1/distance^2, so 20 vs 5 is ~1/16th the fills, and statistical power
collapses. Layers last longer, so they pay MORE carry in absolute terms even
as the ratio improves -- the corrected sweep (4c) is the only way to see
which effect dominates.

**Cheap next step:** extend the grid to widths 9-20 and exits 10-15 on the
ring pairs, same seeds and windows. Infrastructure already exists.
