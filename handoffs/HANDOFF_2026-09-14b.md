## 2. THE I6 HALT -- FOUR WRONG THEORIES, RECORDED DELIBERATELY

GRIND_GBPUSD_OPT halted `I6_LONG_EXIT` at 15:05:40.917 local. It blocked
CloseBy on a netted pair; the operator closed two positions by hand.

**The root cause is STILL UNKNOWN.** What follows is the record of how the
investigation failed, because the pattern matters more than any theory.

### 2a. Theory 1 -- "favourable slippage on a filled exit". WRONG (sign)

A long layer's exit is a SELL LIMIT. It filled BELOW its price, which is
ADVERSE, not favourable. I had the direction backwards and had already
specced ADR-140 to widen I6 asymmetrically on that premise.

**`fix/i6-exit-fill-slippage` is ABANDONED. Do not merge it.** It tested
931/932 and would have MASKED the one event where halting was correct.
Gemini: "Merging a fix based on an inverted premise would systematically
blind the fleet to broker bridge failures."

### 2b. Theory 2 -- "slippage is systematically adverse". WRONG (measured)

Built `--slippage` instead of assuming. Over **358 fills**:

- mean **+0.1 pips** -- slippage is mostly FAVOURABLE
- **4 adverse breaches** beyond the 0.2-pip I6 tolerance out of 204 slipped
  fills, about **2%**
- worst adverse -0.8 (AUDCAD_ALT), best favourable +2.3 (GBPUSD_ALT)
- an outlier: EURGBP_ALT OUT_BY **+7.9 pips** on 2026-09-11, a CloseBy leg

**15:05:40 was a market event, not a broker defect:** five instances across
three pairs filled in the same second with unusually large slippage
(+2.3, +1.7, +1.4, +1.4). Something gapped through several resting orders.

### 2c. Theory 3 -- "enforcement is inconsistent". WRONG (ordering)

Three of the four adverse breaches did NOT halt. Reason: `OnTick` runs
`Grind_ProcessCloseByQueues` (line 228) BEFORE the invariant check (line
232), so in the normal path the pair is netted before I6 ever sees it.

### 2d. Theory 4 -- "the exit fill caused the halt". WRONG (and now settled)

`Grind_ArchiveRecordFill` runs at grind_engine.mqh line 1170, BEFORE the
`if(g_grind_halted) return` at 1171, and `halted_at_receipt` is stamped at
TRANSACTION time, not flush time. The exit fill arrived with
`halted_at_receipt=true`.

**The halt came FIRST. The orphaned exit position is a consequence, not a
cause.** The handler returned at 1171, so the CloseBy pair was never queued.

### 2e. What I could not determine, and why

**Which long layer failed I6, and by how much.** The marker recorded only
the string `I6_LONG_EXIT`. `Grind_ReconCheckInvariants` had the layer index,
entry, exit target, expected value and tolerance at the moment it failed,
and discarded all of it.

Cost: about an hour across `fill_logs`, `ea_events` and the VPS Experts log,
four rejected hypotheses, and no answer.

## 7. OPERATIONAL FINDINGS

**Dragging an order line on an MT5 chart modifies it instantly** -- no
confirmation, no undo. The operator moved EURGBP_ALT's L04 exit from
0.85803 to 0.85854 while trying to maximise a chart; I6 halted the instance
within a tick. **Mitigation: chart Properties -> Show -> untick "Show trade
levels"** on the VPS charts. Orders still work; they just cannot be dragged.

**Reattaching resets in-memory daily counters.** GBPUSD_OPT went from
+7.79/11 scalps to 0.00/0 after its reattach. Use the archive, never the
heartbeat, for daily totals.

**Both arms of a pair log as `fxgrind (GBPUSD,M5)`** with nothing to
distinguish OPT from ALT. ADR-141 adds an instance prefix to EA Prints.

**Detaching is safe only for the book as it stands.** Orders that fill
AFTERWARDS get no exit. Nine positions were left naked on Sunday night.

**A grep for `failed` matches HEARTBEAT lines** (`peer_read_failed`), and a
grep for `HALT` matches them too (`halt_reason`). I hit this twice. The halt
